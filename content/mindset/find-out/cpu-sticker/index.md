It all started around 2018, when I began building PCs because it was the cheapest way to play games with my best friend.

I knew nothing about it, though, and the last thing I wanted was to break components or pick incompatible ones, so I spent a couple of months studying, mostly by watching [Blink46](https://www.youtube.com/@Blink46yt)'s videos.
I became a bit obsessed with them. I think it was because he explained the whole process in detail, from choosing the components to assembling them, all the way to the possible optimizations. I don't follow him anymore, but he taught me a lot.

Since then I've built, repaired and wrecked more computers than I can count.
So many that I got to the point of presuming I knew enough. Until one day I came across something that left me stunned.

On May 20, 2020 (or at least that's what the photo's metadata says), I had to fix the old PC of an engineer friend of mine.
The PC worked; it just ran hot and was generally slow. Nothing a lightly used SSD and fresh thermal paste couldn't fix.
But once I had scraped off the pre-war thermal paste and cleaned the CPU with 90% isopropyl alcohol, I wanted to take the processor out of the socket to show the underside to the engineer, who was watching in amazement.
What I didn't expect was to find a sticker stuck to it.

I remember the engineer saw the look on my face and asked if that was normal, and I, in shock, replied: "No, I'd say this is the most absurd thing I've ever seen."

{{< figure src="cpu-sticker.jpg" alt="The underside of the CPU with a sticker stuck to it" caption="The sticker under the CPU, May 20, 2020." width="400" >}}

The fact that the PC worked with that sticker disturbed me and, at the same time, made me very curious. It made me think that maybe I didn't know enough after all.

Since then I've left behind the "if it works, don't touch it" mindset and moved to "if it works and you don't know why, find out".
Obviously you can't know everything about everything, but since that day I no longer presume to know. Now, when something works and I don't understand why, I get curious, do my research, and often learn something new.

I think this is an essential skill, especially in DevOps.
Because it's when things stop working that everything gets interesting.
